# A creator growth plan: better hours, not more hours

Five music creators, $80,000 and eight weeks to a festival. A take-home case for TikTok LIVE.

- **Context:** an interview case for a Music Creator Manager role. The festival and the five creators are made up. The brief gave the creator data; the targets are my estimates.

## The problem

The brief: TikTok LIVE has signed a partnership with a three-day electronic music festival that draws 100,000+ people. I manage five music creators. Leadership says the five are underperforming.

I had $80,000 and eight weeks to do four things:

- Recruit new music creators to LIVE
- Raise the engagement and income of the current creators
- Show top LIVE talent on site at the festival
- Make LIVE a place people go for live music

## What the data said

The brief gave 30 days of numbers for the five creators. I added one column: revenue per LIVE hour.

| Creator | LIVE hours | Revenue | Revenue per hour | Month on month | Returning viewers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Echo Music | 38 | $12,100 | $318 | +22% | 48% |
| Luna Live | 58 | $17,600 | $303 | +9% | 60% |
| DJ Nova | 115 | $14,200 | $123 | −18% | 81% |
| BeatTheory | 90 | $10,400 | $116 | −7% | 75% |
| Vinyl Nights | 126 | $7,800 | $62 | −20% | 87% |

The two creators who streamed the most were declining the fastest. Two creators made 48% of the revenue in 23% of the hours.

So the problem was not effort. It was what each hour earned. The five creators fell into two patterns:

- **Growing, but viewers do not stay.** Echo grows fastest and has the fewest returning viewers. New people arrive and leave.
- **Loyal, but no new viewers.** Nova and Vinyl have the most returning viewers and the steepest decline. The same fans watch for more hours and spend less.

## What I proposed

### 1. One diagnosis and one plan for each creator

- **Back Echo and Luna.** A fixed schedule, dedicated support for Echo, fan events that turn new viewers into regulars.
- **Fix Nova and Vinyl.** Fewer, better hours (126 down to about 80 for Vinyl), new show formats, and co-streams that bring in new viewers.
- **Hold BeatTheory.** The same pattern as Nova at an early stage. Act now, while the fix is small.

### 2. Use the festival to recruit, not only to celebrate

One program with one line: "From your room to the main stage." It is a competition with two entry points.

- **Musicians who have never gone LIVE** enter with a short video. This opens the door one level below the usual entry rules.
- **Current LIVE creators** compete in their own division and join a four-week training course on streaming and earning.
- **The finals happen on the festival stage** and stream on LIVE. Far more people watch the stream than stand in the crowd, so the stream gets its own line-up and hosts.

The prizes are career steps, not cash: a stage slot, distribution support, and the winner's own track as a limited gift on the platform.

### 3. Put the money where it adds up

| Where | Budget |
|---|---|
| Stage and production | $28,000 |
| Prizes and travel | $18,000 |
| Marketing | $16,000 |
| Training course | $8,000 |
| On-site operations | $4,000 |
| Reserve | $6,000 |

More than half goes to the stage and the competition. Each one makes the other worth more.

### 4. Split the plan in two: committed and stretch

- **Committed:** runs on confirmed resources only. A competition and a final on music that is cleared in week 1.
- **Stretch:** happens only if other teams and partners say yes. An artist collaboration, custom event effects, a push to take one show past 5,000 live viewers.

### 5. Measure against a baseline, and decide in week 4

Every target compares the campaign weeks with a seven-day average from before, with the outlier days removed. Early signals decide the spend: 500+ entries and 150+ first-time LIVE creators. In week 4, the numbers say go on, or move the money.

## How it works

1. **Weeks 1 to 2: check and launch.** Check each diagnosis against internal data in a 1:1 with the creator. Open entries. Clear the music rights.
2. **Weeks 3 to 6: compete and train.** Weekly rounds, the training course, fan clip missions. The week 4 checkpoint decides the rest of the budget.
3. **Weeks 7 to 8: the stage.** Rehearsal, the final on site, QR codes that link each stage and booth to a creator's LIVE. Four weeks later, a report on who stayed.

## What I learned

### A target without a baseline is a guess

My first draft put "ROI 5 to 6×" on the cover. I had no internal baseline behind it. I moved the calculation to the method page, showed how I got to 5×, and marked it as an estimate to check in week 1.

### Say which parts depend on other people

My first draft treated product support, artists and music rights as available. None of them was mine to promise. The committed and stretch split came from that. A plan is easier to approve when the reader sees what is certain.

### Read hours and revenue together

Hours alone made Vinyl and Nova look like the hardest workers. Revenue alone made Luna look like the only star. Revenue per hour showed the real picture in one column.
